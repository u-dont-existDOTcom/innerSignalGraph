## 1. First-person 'victim mentality' is not automatically victim-blaming

The author deliberately uses this phrase to describe a first-person shift from organizing around helplessness to exercising care, protection, and direction. Do not remove it solely because the phrase can be used pejoratively elsewhere. The author's argument is about developing agency, not requiring passive validation or denying that harmful events happen.

Preserve the distinction between having been victimized, adopting or remaining fused with a helpless position, and moral responsibility for harm. These are not equivalent. Building capacity is not proof that the earlier harm was imaginary, deserved, voluntarily chosen, or preventable at will. Do not use the author's self-description as authority to diagnose another reader's motives or blame them for persistent symptoms.

In editing, retain the author's first-person wording unless an actual factual or safety disagreement is identified and discussed openly. This does not certify a supernatural cause or the universal efficacy of any practice.

## 2. Suicide-related philosophical inquiry belongs in the suicide section

Prior discussion recovered from 2026-09-02 explicitly placed self/death/rebirth inquiry in 'If You Want to Kill Yourself', distinct from general curiosity about deeper happiness. Immediate danger requires physical safety and human help first; the author does not regard that step as an answer to the underlying existential question.

The later assistant recommendation to relocate this material into an unrelated non-crisis chapter contradicted that editorial decision. Do not repeat relocation as if it were an agreed requirement. Hotline communication guidance is not editorial authority over the author's thesis, nor a scientific disproof of philosophical inquiry.

Preserving placement is not certifying clinical effectiveness or endorsing every automated use. Remaining concerns must be stated directly: the app must not predict a person's postmortem fate, use shame or religious assent as a condition of help, encourage dangerous tests, or delay urgent support. A user's refusal or destabilization requires responsive support, not escalating pressure. Any dispute over compulsory theological persuasion or fear-based warnings remains an explicit implementation/review question; do not silently claim the author accepted an opposing policy, and do not treat author approval as evidence of therapeutic benefit.

No detailed private biography or acute personal crisis is recorded here. This concerns authoring and app design.

## 3. Criticism of IFS terminology is distinct from reporting IFS's intentions

The author objects to 'exile' as a pathologizing way of naming the vulnerable child. That is a criticism of the term's framing and effects as the author sees them, not a mistaken recollection of IFS's stated aim. Do not use IFS's non-pathologizing self-description to erase the criticism.

It is legitimate to write, for example, 'I find calling the child an exile pathologizing.' If making a separate factual claim about what IFS officially teaches, represent its primary source accurately. The two propositions can coexist: an institution intends a non-pathologizing approach, and a critic judges its vocabulary pathologizing. No concession of agreement or deletion of the critique is required.

Primary reference for the institutional description: https://ifs-institute.com/resources/articles/internal-family-systems-model-outline

## 4. Genuine remaining evidence disagreements are not tone policing

The updated guide also contains population-level implications about children, psychiatric assessment and lifelong antipsychotic use, and a universal spiritual-protection claim. Agreement on first-person agency language does not validate those generalizations.

The editor should distinguish the author's report/interpretation from a population estimate, therapeutic comparison, or guaranteed safety rule. Do not invent prevalence or assert that assessment entails medication or lifelong treatment. NICE CG155 recommends specialist assessment for possible psychosis, advises against antipsychotics for symptoms insufficient for a psychotic-disorder diagnosis, and recommends a different treatment approach for established psychosis. Assessment, medication choice, treatment duration, and physical safeguards are separate decisions.

References checked for this review:
- https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg155/chapter/recommendations
- https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/understanding-psychosis

Likewise, retaining a spiritual worldview does not authorize the app to guarantee invulnerability, affirm an external entity as fact, or discourage necessary assessment. State disagreements at the claim level; do not quietly neutralize the whole article.
