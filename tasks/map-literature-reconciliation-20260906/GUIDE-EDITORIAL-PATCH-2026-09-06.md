## E01 — Helper disagreement

Section: **Don’t Give the Inner Adult Away**. Insert immediately before the paragraph beginning “Borrow adulthood. Do not surrender it.”

> Disagreement with a helper is not automatically a defense against healing. Their interpretation may be wrong, their method may not fit, or you may not share their goal. They should be able to examine that without making your objection prove their theory.

Basis: MI2023, sustain talk versus discord; MENTAL2008, chapter 6. This does not make every objection correct or prohibit a well-founded challenge.

## E02 — A real non-engagement option

Section: **Also Look Outward**. Insert after its closing paragraph: “The Witness notices what is happening inside me. The Protector notices what is happening between us. The Guide asks: ‘Given both, what is the next sane move?’” Keep the existing section unchanged. New subsection before **Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult**:

### When More Processing Becomes the Hook

A real wound and an unhelpful response to it can coexist. You may still have grief to feel while repeatedly checking whether you have grieved correctly. Attend to immediate safety and practical action when needed, but another round of analysis is not automatically more care.

Ask: “Is this helping me feel or understand something more clearly, make a decision, or respond differently—or am I checking the same question for reassurance?” Meaningful grief and practice can revisit familiar ground; novelty is not required every time. But repetition alone does not make a thought a new request from the child.

When it is a checking loop, let the thought be there without arguing with it, chasing it away, or waiting to feel completely resolved. Return to a chosen activity or relationship. You can care for the child without holding a meeting every time the mind produces a sentence.

The exercises here should not become a ritual you must complete before you are allowed to live.

Basis: MCT2009, pp.1–22,71–88,161–162,192–193; BA2022 chapter 7. This is selective integration, not a complete MCT protocol. Meaningful repeated practice and grief are not automatically rumination, and unresolved material is not declared healed.

## E03 — Responsive care, not just the right words

Section: **Borrow Love—or Borrow the Perspective of Care**. Insert after the paragraph: “Don’t force the transfer. Even recognizing that the capacity exists somewhere in you is useful. You are borrowing a familiar pathway rather than manufacturing a feeling on command.”

> Notice not just the words, but how they are being said inside. “I care about you” can carry patience or a demand to hurry up and feel better. If imagined closeness feels intrusive, try more distance, no touch, a different voice, or another source of care. Difficulty with this particular offer does not establish an inability to receive love. Let the response teach you how to care, rather than forcing the child to accept your preferred version.

Basis: CFT2010, pp.6–8,128; ATTACH2016, responsive and individualized attunement. Does not duplicate the already-present giving/receiving distinction or redefine love as non-cruelty alone.

## E04 — Spiritual struggle and mature relinquishment

Section: **Ask God for a Loan**. Insert after its final paragraph beginning “This is the feast problem.” New subsection before **Become the Adult Apprentice**:

### When the Spiritual Relationship Hurts

Sometimes the difficulty is not simply accessing love. Prayer may evoke abandonment or condemnation; a teacher’s betrayal may damage trust in what once felt sacred. Don’t automatically call this bypass, lack of faith, or disguised parental conflict. Ask what has been lost, what needs questioning, and what is still worth preserving. You may need to grieve or change a relationship, practice, or understanding. This need not require either abandoning spirituality or forcing more of the same.

Nor does developing an inner adult mean controlling everything yourself. Spiritual support can help you act where responsibility is yours and relinquish outcomes or other people’s choices where control is not yours.

Basis: PARG2007, pp.12–23 and chapters7–8,10–15. Spiritual struggle is not assumed to be bypass or parental projection. Continued devotion remains compatible with development. No metaphysical-protection law is inferred from this literature.

## E05 — Delight as a Nurturer function

Section: **The Three Adult Functions**. Append to the Nurturer paragraph immediately after “The Nurturer makes room for what is true without attacking the person who feels it.”

> The Nurturer also takes pleasure in the child’s company: “I’m glad you’re here.” The child needs to be enjoyed, not only comforted when hurt or managed when difficult.

Basis: ATTACH2016, expressed delight and supported exploration, pp.291–292. Makes the already-valued vitality operational without forcing affection when unavailable.

## E06 — Plan and review the actual action

Section: **Make the Protector Visible**. Add at the end, after “Warmth can come later.” and before **Love Doesn’t Have to Wait for Trust**:

> Make one chosen act specific: when will you do it, how small can the first step be, and what support or resources does it need? A brief rehearsal may help with a predictable difficult moment. Afterward, review what actually happened. Not doing it may reflect fear, uncertainty about the goal, lack of skill, exhaustion, or a practical barrier; don’t decide which without looking. Completing it without immediate relief is not automatically failure, and completion alone does not prove it helped. Adjust the action to what it actually contributes to safety, connection, competence, or enjoyment—not just to whether it earns a tick.

Basis: BA2022, chapters4–5,8–9, especially143–145; MI2023, focusing/confidence/planning; SCHEMA2003 chapter5. Early safe action in this guide is an integration, not the exact sequence in the Schema manual. Not a compulsory homework or productivity system.

## E07 — Follow the change point

Section: **A Bottom-Up Sequence**. Insert after the paragraph beginning “Only after the protective layer feels heard does the inner child often come forward.” and ending “while nothing underneath feels safer.”

> The protector does not have to disappear. It may remain watchful while willingly allowing one small step, provided you remain present and able to stop. If it says no, don’t turn that into permission.
>
> Then follow what changes. A critic may soften into a fear; a vague hurt may become a specific need; anger may clarify a boundary that needs action. Respond to what has become available instead of restarting the original exercise. When a need becomes clear, try offering that kind of care and notice how it lands. You don’t need an age, a recovered memory, or a complete cast of parts to work with a feeling. A useful shift may be small; more intensity is not the only way forward.

Basis: EMOTION2025, pp.141–149 and chapters10–14; CFT2010, responsive nurturance. An adaptation of task/change-point concepts, not a claim that all source therapies use this exact guard-permission rule. Does not create a mandatory census or unanimous agreement ritual among inner parts.

## E08 — Overcontrol is not developed adulthood

Section: **The Inner Guide Comes Later**. Insert after the paragraph: “Without direction, reparenting can become permissive. The child receives attention but doesn’t develop. Candy, screens, and escape from anything difficult can masquerade as self-love.”

> The opposite trap is forced adulthood: perfect discipline, no visible needs, and very little joy or closeness. In that case the next adult act may be asking for help, expressing a preference, or letting a safe person see something imperfect—not tightening control again. Judge the strategy as well as the need. Wanting closeness is legitimate; demanding instant replies or punishing someone for needing space is not the same thing. The Guide helps find a workable way to meet the need without erasing either person.

Basis: RODBT2018, openness/flexibility/connection; SCHEMA2003, needs versus coping and empathic confrontation. Not a diagnosis based on introversion or culturally reserved expression. Preserves accountability.

## E09 — Separate care-evoked grief from an unestablished physiological explanation

Section: **Somatic Heart Opening**. Leave the preceding Rumi sentence and following overwhelm warning unchanged in this patch. Replace only:

> The original mechanism language here locates the feeling around the ventral-vagal system and cardiac plexus: some people find that deep grieving eventually drops the nervous system into social engagement and gives way to a physical sense of being held by the universe—a biological rebound.

With:

> Some people find that deep grieving gives way to warmth, connection, or a bodily sense of being held by the universe. That describes an experience, not evidence for one particular physiological mechanism. Love can also open grief in the other direction: receiving care may bring sadness for the care that was missing. That need not mean the love failed. Stay with it when you can remain present and retain choice; more tears are not, by themselves, proof of more healing.

Basis: CFT2010, pp.202–204; EMOTION2025 chapter14. Explicit evidence correction: these sources do not establish the exact ventral-vagal/cardiac-plexus biological-rebound explanation. The reported possibility of an experience is retained, not certified or disproven. This edit does not newly verify the Rumi attribution.

## E11 — Bound repeated forgiveness inquiry without changing its aim

Section: **How to Forgive Without Forgetting**. Replace the final sentence:

> Stay with the inquiry until the understanding becomes real enough to change the emotional relationship.

With:

> Return to the inquiry over time, without forcing it or endlessly rehearsing the same case, until the understanding becomes real enough to change the emotional relationship.

This preserves the author’s causal-understanding/forgiveness argument while bringing its timing into alignment with E02. It does not claim Wells endorses the full theory of forgiveness.
