## Corrections applied
1. Delivery is a real boundary: an unsent suggestion is not advice already received. Future replies must refer only to confirmed deliveries.
2. In multi-topic replies group each subject into one contiguous block. When the main therapeutic issue is the primary question to answer, keep the complete evidence, practical inquiry, and question together, often at the end. Do not open the issue, detour to other topics and reopen it. Safety, consent and deliberate agenda changes override placement.
3. Check longitudinal attempts and journal evidence before default advice on cessation or withdrawal. Ease of stopping is different from reasons for returning. Understand what a risky coping choice reliably supplies and what non-harmful, affordable, feasible option could meet some of that need. Account for limited capacity to organize a full routine or control social access.
4. For an experience described as intensely alive, ask directly what aspect matters most and which non-harmful experiences have already provided some of it, rather than generating speculative explanations.
5. A partial body shift such as easier shoulder tension is not proof subjective anxiety disappeared. Observed social calm is not evidence of absent internal distress. Find which practice helps, for how long, what happens when continuing or stopping it, and how effects transfer into life. Preserve reports of both improvement and worsening.
6. Preserve inclusive group references in all language transformations: monastics means monks and nuns, not monks alone. Preserve attribution and uncertainty.
