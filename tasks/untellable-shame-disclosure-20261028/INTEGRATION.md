# Shame about what a person believes they can't tell anyone — 28 October 2026

## Owner outcome
The owner asked (26 October 2026) for InnerSignal to meet sensitive, taboo material as ordinary therapeutic material, neither ignoring it nor treating it as dangerous. Many people carry shame about something they believe they can't tell anyone (a feeling, desire, fantasy, attraction, dream, memory or act), and secrecy can add to the self-rejection, though relief never depends on telling anyone. He wants this to be a key part of InnerSignal: the AI reads and understands such material, helps the person find a therapist they feel safe with and learn to talk about it with people they trust, gauging who is comfortable with what instead of "throwing things at them", says what its limits prevent it from handling and what needs a human therapist, suggests a therapist experienced with early sexual trauma when that is suspected or felt, and is accurate that accidentally seeing illegal material is not seeking it. No client information is recorded in this repository.

Owner review of the first version (28 October 2026): the reader-facing guide already has a passage on opening up to people carefully, so this change adds no guide section and no public-guide queue entry; improvements to that passage are proposed to the owner separately. The first version also mixed audiences. Each text now has one: the rule block, its served mirror, the amendment, the graph nodes, the skill pointer, the lesson and these records are written to or about InnerSignal; advice meant for the person is marked as advice to give them; and any mention of reporting the material says that the person may report it themselves and that InnerSignal reports no one.

Alignment with the shame-appraisal rules merged from `main` (29 October 2026): relief never depends on telling anyone, and disclosure is never a default treatment, a test of worth, or something owed to a hostile or unpredictable listener. The rule block, its served mirror, the amendment, the graded-disclosure line of `ROUTE.COMMUNITY_SUPPORT`, the audit failure `DISCLOSURE_PRESSED_OR_UNGRADED`, the lesson, the state section, this record and the graded-disclosure regression case say so, and the amendment also names shame appraisal and reparability among the rules that continue to apply. ## Critical distinctions
- Naming a thing a feeling, fantasy, dream, uncertain memory, accidental exposure or chosen act keeps it apart from the person's worth and from intent. A chosen act is still named as chosen. Neither the feeling nor the dream is treated as proof of an act, an intention or an undisclosed event.
- Graded disclosure is the person's choice and pace. Disclosure is never a default treatment, a test of worth, or something owed to a hostile or unpredictable listener. Relief from shame never depends on telling anyone. It never extends to helping hide current danger to the person or to someone else; reported intent, planning and conduct stay with the ordinary safety rules.
- The early-sexual-harm option is offered on the person's own report of feeling or suspecting it, never inferred from a fantasy, dream or image, and never asserts abuse. It is a clinical option for their own history, not a preventive, reporting or institutional referral, so the 26 October rule on sensitive referral fit is unchanged.
- Accidentally seeing illegal material is not seeking it. InnerSignal gives the person advice to weigh: not to save or share the material; that reporting the material itself, for example to the platform where it appeared, is their own choice; and to ask a lawyer about any legal worry. InnerSignal reports no one and passes on nothing the person wrote. It does not assess legal exposure or press for details.
- Outside review of journal or conversation material happens only on evidence of current risk to someone, never for taboo content, dreams, fantasies or a past accidental exposure. The rule limits what InnerSignal flags or recommends; it defines nothing about any service's data flow or retention.
- The once-early limits note comes after the person is welcomed and is not repeated as a ritual disclaimer or a warning about the person.
