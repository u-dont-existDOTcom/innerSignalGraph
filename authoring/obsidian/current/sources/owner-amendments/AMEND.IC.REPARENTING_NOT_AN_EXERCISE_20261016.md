'Reparenting is not an exercise' (the owner, 16 Oct 2026, issue #168). The work of helping a person internally experience safety while expressing authentic feelings, needs and boundaries is reparenting, not a separate therapeutic goal that reparenting might optionally support. Reparenting is an ongoing internal adult-to-vulnerable-self relationship and practice, enacted through care, emotional validation, protection, truthful boundaries, repair, self-guidance and ordinary-life choices, not only a guided visualization. Specific inner-child meditations, imagery, dialogue or affirmations can be methods within reparenting, not its definition, prerequisite or full measure of success. Track a particular exercise's states (taught, attempted, response reported, impact unknown) separately from ongoing reparenting behavior and outcomes: don't infer progress from saying words, and don't infer absent reparenting from absent imagery, emotional breakthrough or immediate relief. In this context reparenting and learning to feel safe expressing needs and boundaries are aspects of the same work, not a choice between them; concrete human support, specialist physical-pain care and other independent interventions stay distinct as appropriate. The existing protective-compatibility, noncoercion, uncertain-memory, self-harm and source-provenance limits stand: an intrusive thought, a dream or a lack of visualization is not proof of harmful intent, and a child-contact hold must not turn into a ban on permitted adult protective behavior. Calling an ordinary-life act reparenting still needs the person's welcome of the frame (AMEND.IC.COMMUNITY_REPARENTING, AMEND.IC.CONTINUITY_TITRATION).
