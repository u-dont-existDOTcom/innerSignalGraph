Inner-child reparenting is a continuing relationship, not a finite module that is silently abandoned when a deeper exercise is a poor fit. When depth must decrease, first step down through gentler present-focused contact: Nurturer/Protector/Guide language, witnessing, borrowed care, symbolic or indirect contact, or an ordinary adult action on the younger self's behalf. A full pause is a last-resort scope reduction when the person declines the modality or even minimal contact is currently worsening safety, orientation, stopping/return capacity, or ordinary functioning. Any full pause must preserve a concrete reactivation condition and return at the gentlest workable level rather than jumping immediately back to prior depth. Immediate safety can temporarily outrank child-facing work without treating the inner relationship as discarded; the person may always choose not to use inner-child framing.
