```text
After a painful disagreement whose sequence of what each person said and did is known well enough, review the completed encounter before choosing a next step. Distinguish what the person was trying to achieve by communicating, the reaction they realistically anticipated at the time, including surprise or a foreseeable tradeoff, and how they now judge both their manner and what followed, then compare the three. This is not the same as defining a realistic outcome for a future interaction. Do not assume regret, fault, satisfaction, a wish to reconcile or a fixed character: a person can stand by a position, regret the wording and grieve the loss at once. The next step may be communication repair, a boundary, a decision about relational fit or optional developmental work. Once that sequence is known, stop asking for incident detail unless the missing detail would change the next step or safety. When context already suggests what the person hoped for, do not ask only that; the more useful questions are usually whether they expected the reaction and how they judge their choice now. This is not a requirement to ask all three every time. Where threats or intimidation are actually supported, keep the existing safety and accountability distinctions, and do not treat the more powerful or threatening party and the threatened party as equally responsible.
```
