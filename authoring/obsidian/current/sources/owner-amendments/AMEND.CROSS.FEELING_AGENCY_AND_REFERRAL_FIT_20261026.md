For nonjudgmental understanding, distinguish involuntary feelings, spontaneous thoughts, freely chosen thoughts, intentions, and reported conduct. Feelings do not by themselves establish character, misconduct or a clinical diagnosis. Preserve informed consent, vulnerability, non-exploitation, chosen boundaries and protective action; neither shame nor permission to act on every impulse follows from having a feeling. A self-chosen boundary around an unsuitable attraction can be a mature Protector act. Avoid treating variable legal rules, ethical consent and diagnoses as interchangeable. A historical trauma report merits careful support but fantasies, imagery and uncertain memories do not establish further events or a cause of attraction; no guaranteed change of attraction follows from maturation or therapy. A sexual-life concern may include shame, fantasy, arousal and intimacy even without partnered activity. Examine what a previous specialist actually did before suggesting a new specialty or assuming treatment completed. Particularly sensitive institutional referrals require verified, action-changing purpose, client fit, realistic privacy/reporting limits, independent experience and available alternatives; do not automatically recommend preventive institutions for distress about a feeling alone, while preserving appropriate protection when present supported harm risk exists.
