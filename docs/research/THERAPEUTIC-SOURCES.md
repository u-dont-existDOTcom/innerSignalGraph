# Therapeutic sources and intellectual contributions

Recorded 2026-09-06 against PR #46 at `046614b045d4a15ea71b3b61e74b11d6b615a2ed`.

This is a contribution record, not a list of endorsements. Consulted, convergent, adopted, candidate-implemented, behavior-tested and deployed are different statuses. A source can contain several ideas with different statuses. The owner's original framework and direct contributors remain credited separately from books read later.

## Ten directly consulted books

Relevant conceptual, treatment and troubleshooting chapters were read. This is not a claim that every page was read, that every cited study was independently reverified, or that each entire manual has been converted into a chatbot protocol. R1-R6 refer to `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-06-map-literature-reconciliation.md`. Newly proposed refinements in that document are not enacted product policy. No target-model behavioral run or app-level clinical validation has occurred in this task.

### MI2023 — Motivational Interviewing: Helping People Change and Grow

William R. Miller and Stephen Rollnick. Fourth edition, 2023. Guilford Press. ISBN 9781462552795.

Locations: chapters 1-2 (engaging, focusing, evoking, planning and collaboration); chapters 4 and 7 (affirmation, commitment and forced promises); chapters 9-12, 14 and 17 (task guidance, information/advice, discord).

Already present: the snapshot has `user_goal` and `current_issue`; incremental extraction accepts corrections. Contribution: finer task selection, not mistaking a practical barrier for ambivalence, and repairing the helping interaction. R2/R3. Later direct textbook review, not an origin claim for the founder framework. No full MI implementation or certification claimed.

### CFT2010 — Compassion Focused Therapy: Distinctive Features

Paul Gilbert. First edition, 2010. Routledge. ISBN 9780415448079.

Locations: printed pp.6-8 (felt manner versus logical content); pp.101-107 (responsibility and compassionate correction); pp.128-136 and155-170 (nurturance, compassion directions, cultivated compassionate self); pp.197-207 (wellbeing, play, fear of compassion and grief).

Already present: `IC.BORROW_LOVE` distinguishes love from safety of receiving; `IC.CREDIBILITY_REPAIR` already preserves nondefensive accountability and actual evidence. Contribution: responsive manner, the specific barrier to receiving, grief emerging with care, and vitality. R3/R5. This is substantial later conceptual convergence, not proof of the whole synthesis or a claim to deliver CFT.

### PARG2007 — Spiritually Integrated Psychotherapy: Understanding and Addressing the Sacred

Kenneth I. Pargament. First edition, 2007. Guilford Press. ISBN 9781572308442.

Locations: printed pp.12-23 (resources, struggles, values and nonreductionism); pp.61-63 and Figure3.1 p.62 (discovery, conservation, transformation); chapters10-15, particularly pp.202-213,289-290,311-312 (assessment, spiritual struggle, surrender and control).

Already present: continuing devotion is protected by `ROUTE.INFLUENCE_LOVE_CAPACITY`; realization is distinguished from trustworthiness by `IC.REALIZATION_LOVE_INTEGRATION`. Contribution: work with spiritual struggle as its own problem and distinguish appropriate surrender from abandoned responsibility. R6. The sacred is not reduced to a coping instrument. No metaphysical or supernatural-protection claim is established by this source review.

### SCHEMA2003 — Schema Therapy: A Practitioner's Guide

Jeffrey E. Young, Janet S. Klosko and Marjorie E. Weishaar. First edition, copyright 2003. Guilford Press. ISBN 9781572308381. The supplied filename says 2007; the actual copyright/title-page basis is 2003.

Locations: pp.9-10 (postulated needs and explicit limits of that list's evidence in this edition); pp.32-40/Table1.1 (coping responses); chapters5-6,pp.146-206 (pattern breaking and therapeutic relationship); chapter8,pp.271-305 (Healthy Adult/child modes and transfer).

Already present: the founder adult/child framework predates this comparison; guard and credibility nodes examine coping and responsibility. Contribution: distinguish need from strategy, compassionate confrontation and generalization beyond a session. R3/R4/R5. Later convergence and selected adaptation, not retroactive authorship. Do not import compulsory inventories, attachment leverage for attendance, or a war against an internal person as automatic app defaults.

### MCT2009 — Metacognitive Therapy for Anxiety and Depression

Adrian Wells. First edition, 2009. Guilford Press. ISBN 9781593859947.

Locations: pp.1-22 and36-41 (CAS, beliefs about thinking, process/content); pp.56-69 (attention training); pp.71-88 (detached mindfulness and its misuse); pp.161-162 and192-193 (ritual stopping criteria).

Already present: `ROUTE.LEAVE_ALONE`, `ROUTE.THREE_WAY_GATE`, `ROUTE.EXTERNAL_EMBODIMENT`. Contribution: repairing process-versus-topic routing and preventing a therapeutic exercise from becoming compulsory neutralizing/checking. R1/R4/R5. Metacognitive ideas already motivated the route; the full textbook subsequently sharpened it. ATT is not generic grounding and this does not establish delivery of complete MCT.

### ATTACH2016 — Attachment Disturbances in Adults: Treatment for Comprehensive Repair

Daniel P. Brown and David S. Elliott. First edition, 2016. W. W. Norton. ISBN 9780393711523.

Locations: chapters7-10 (three pillars, ideal-parent imagery, metacognitive development, collaboration); chapter8 and pp.287-292 (protection, attunement, comfort, delight and exploration); chapters11-13 (adaptations).

Already present: `IC.BORROW_ONE_FUNCTION`, `IC.ADULT_APPRENTICE`, `IC.IDENTITY_FORMATION`, `IC.GUIDE_LATER`. Contribution: repair a mismatch in care, include delight rather than duty alone, and support exploration. R1/R5. Their fictional Ideal Parent Figures are not identical to all borrowing from real, remembered or spiritual sources. The app does not inherit clinician-level competence from reading the manual.

### MENTAL2008 — Mentalizing in Clinical Practice

Jon G. Allen, Peter Fonagy and Anthony W. Bateman. First edition, 2008. American Psychiatric Publishing. ISBN 9781585623068.

Locations: chapters2-6 (mentalizing, current capacity, contingent responsiveness, prementalizing states and interventions); chapter7 (attachment trauma).

Already present: observation/hypothesis separation, distinct witness capacity and intuition/trust calibration. Contribution: lower interpretive complexity when understanding becomes rigid or disconnected; correct the app's own misunderstanding. R2/R3/R5. Eloquence is not evidence of current flexibility; text does not reveal nonverbal process. No automatic diagnostic classification.

### RODBT2018 — Radically Open Dialectical Behavior Therapy: Theory and Practice for Treating Disorders of Overcontrol

Thomas R. Lynch. First edition, 2018. Context Press/New Harbinger. ISBN 9781626259287.

Locations: chapters1,3-9 (overcontrol, private/public expression, social connection); chapter7 (openness/self-enquiry); Table9.2 (social signaling).

Already present: Guide/differentiation/identity work protects vitality and warns against punitive adulthood. Contribution: choose flexibility and connection rather than more inhibition when rigid overcontrol is the actual problem. R4/R5. No full RO DBT program, diagnosis from introversion, prescribed cultural conformity, or inference of facial expression from chat.

### BA2022 — Behavioral Activation for Depression: A Clinician's Guide

Christopher R. Martell, Sona Dimidjian and Ruth Herman-Dunn. Second edition, 2022. Guilford Press. ISBN 9781462548385.

Locations: pp.20-32 (individualized principles); chapter4,pp.62-88 (functional assessment); chapter5,pp.89-108 (graded scheduling and natural reinforcement); chapters6-8,pp.109-160 (avoidance, rumination, troubleshooting); chapter9,pp.161-174 (progress and termination).

Already present: `actionable_problem` includes behavior; outward/Protector/apprentice routes prescribe small useful actions. Contribution: cue/size/barrier/consequence planning and review of actual attempts. R1/R4. Activity is not automatically rewarding, rest is not automatically avoidance, and BA can include approaching grief. Do not call every outward suggestion a complete BA treatment.

### EMOTION2025 — Learning Emotion-Focused Therapy: A Comprehensive Guide

Robert Elliott, Jeanne C. Watson, Rhonda N. Goldman and Leslie S. Greenberg. Second edition, 2025. American Psychological Association. ISBN 9781433838323; PDF ISBN 9781433849176. Use copyright/publication 2025, not the catalogue's 2026 classification field.

Locations: pp.55-68 (emotion responses); pp.141-149/Table6.1 pp.144-146 (marker, intervention, change point, task process); chapter8,pp.211-224 (relationship repair/contact); chapters10-12 (unclear feelings, narrative/meaning, self-criticism/interruption); chapter13,pp.391-400 (unfinished relationships); chapter14,pp.403-422,especially404-405 (coping versus transformational self-soothing); pp.476-485 (session endings).

Already present: actual-material requirement and readiness gates. Contribution: current emotional task and change-point guidance within existing routes, not simply permission to go deep. R1/R2/R3/R5. Emotion-focused deepening and Wells's non-engagement are different rationales, not identical operations. This source must use the unambiguous label `emotion_focused`: existing `SOM.EFT_PORTABLE` is Emotional Freedom Techniques/tapping.

## Other contributors, traditions and comparisons

- **Owner's original framework:** adult/child integration, Nurturer/Protector/Guide, borrowed adulthood, love versus trust, spiritual horizon and revisability. Keep its original authorship and openly distinguish later clinical comparisons.
- **Key, as credited in the owner guide:** Guide function, identity and Bowen-derived differentiation. Direct contributor; do not invent a surname, credential or broader endorsement.
- **Daja, as credited in the owner guide:** Nurturer/Protector archetypal work and Three Adults framing. Exact book/training bibliography still requires confirmation; do not invent it.
- **Lisa Romano:** a named practice resource in the guide. No complete Romano book reviewed here; no blanket endorsement.
- **Lindsay C. Gibson:** selected direct adaptation through author/publisher material and earlier review: demonstrated relational capacity, emotional takeover, self-possession and realistic outcomes. Disentangling from Emotionally Immature People was recommended, not supplied/read in full here. Preserve uncertainty about inability versus unwillingness; do not diagnose others or infer immaturity from disagreement.
- **Murray Bowen:** differentiation credited by the owner via Key. Mediated conceptual influence; no full Bowen text reviewed here.
- **Eugene T. Gendlin:** Focusing named in the guide and discussed in the emotion-focused source. Felt sense and working distance; no separate full-book review claimed.
- **Pema Chödrön:** shenpa, worldly-concern pairs and returning authority are directly used in the owner guide. Link exact publications before quoting in promotional material; this record does not invent a title.
- **Richard C. Schwartz / IFS:** selected guard-work overlap and explicit theoretical/terminological disagreement. Do not describe the product as certified/full IFS or erase the owner's criticism of exile terminology.
- **Robert Falconer:** practitioner discussion of unattached burdens considered as a comparator. No full-book review or validated entity-detection/removal claim.
- **Julian Palmer:** practitioner accounts considered; Torping explicitly not adopted. Do not list it as a delivered technique or assert comparative efficacy from anecdotes.
- **Buddhist sources and traditions:** direct spiritual framework. Discussed references include AN4.67, the mettā-benefits discourse, MN21, DN32 and MN129. Canonical text, commentary, founder interpretation and clinical evidence are different categories; no monolithic tradition or clinical-efficacy inference.
- **Christian sources and traditions:** user-framed love/prayer resources. Do not assert Christianity establishes an empirical law comparing spiritual and physical protection.
- **Ajahn Brahm / Peace Pilgrim and other practitioner accounts:** owner-reported illustrations. Exact stories/publications were not recovered in this audit; no invented verified quotations.
- **Somatic source family:** the guide and current somatic graph name Somatic Experiencing, tapping, EMDR, Brainspotting, gentle movement/qigong, yoga and breathing. A named method is not evidence that its founder's full textbook was reviewed. Add exact authors/books when individually verified rather than backfilling presumed influences.

## Using this record publicly

Suggested wording:

> InnerSignal's development brings an original inner-adult/inner-child framework into dialogue with work on compassion, attachment, behavioral change, metacognition, emotion processing, relationships and spiritual integration. We document the particular ideas considered or adapted, the places we disagree, and which features have actually been implemented and evaluated.

For unintegrated features use 'reviewed during development' or 'proposed adaptation', not 'InnerSignal delivers this therapy'. Later convergence is not retroactive evidence that a book caused the original design. No listed author or institution is represented as endorsing, supervising, certifying or partnering with the app. Human-delivered therapy research does not automatically validate an AI adaptation or the whole app.

No source PDFs/EPUBs, long extracts, textbook clinical transcripts, proprietary forms or full-text retrieval indexes are published here. Bibliographic credit does not itself grant permission to reproduce those materials. App prompts/examples should be original. Per-contribution implementation and evaluation status should be updated with exact commits and real results, not by moving an entire author's name into an 'evidence-based' marketing list.
