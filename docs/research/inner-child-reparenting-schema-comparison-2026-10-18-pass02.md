## Decision summary

**Convergence:** Care, protection, guidance, the Happy Child, therapist/mentor modeling, progressively internalized Healthy Adult, actual behavioral boundaries, compassionate engagement with defenses, child receiving care, independent practice, and shared delight are already parts of Schema Therapy. Do not claim that the owner's guide invented these.

**Potential distinctiveness:** The guide's three *named* adult functions and sequencing, its explicit definition of personal protective acts as reparenting, its nonpathologizing refusal/right to reject the frame, provisional interpretations of parts, the strict distinction between meaning and recovered fact, and the longer spiritual horizon constitute an owner's practice synthesis; whether any element is genuinely new relative to all earlier inner-child literature is **unestablished**. A specific integration can be personally original without being historically unprecedented or independently efficacy-validated.

**Best high-value additions to investigate, not yet authorized to publish:** (1) a compact, real-world relapse/response menu; (2) an example showing how one fear produces surrender/avoidance/inversion and different needed adult responses; (3) an optional future-situation rehearsal example with actual choices, limits and review; (4) if needed, explicit therapist-repair criteria and autonomy granting versus autonomy support. Their utility must be checked against the full current master before declaring them missing.

**Preserve differences:** Do not require the client to prove a no is an avoidant part, make trauma-memory imagery mandatory, wait weeks to address immediate safety, require twenty-plus labeled parts, treat metaphor/imagery as historical evidence, convert a book method into unsupervised PTSD treatment, or imply that a therapeutic model substitutes for medical/practical support.

## Findings carried from Pass 01 (F01–F08)

**F01 — Adult/child foundation already covered.** Van Genderen printed pp. 58–60, 103–107, 151–154 and 183–184 describes the Healthy Adult nurturing/protecting/guiding the vulnerable child, letting the Happy Child flourish, external caregiver coaching, and independent adult action. In the 2020 collection, chapter 6's three-stage imagery proceeds from therapist support to client-as-adult intervention to experiencing that intervention as the child. This is extremely close to the guide's borrowed adult/received care. Classification: **already present, no new claim**.

**F02 — Seven Positive Parenting Patterns as an audit lens.** Heath/Startup chapter 4 (Lockwood and Samson) introduces emotional nurturance and unconditional love; playfulness/emotional openness; autonomy support; autonomy granting; dependability; intrinsic worth; and confidence/competence. Each has at least a counterpart within Nurturer/Protector/Guide, developmental play and the guide's autonomy emphasis. Distinguish encouraging competence from granting genuine choice/refusal. The underlying empirical associations are with remembered parenting and functioning, **not proof that therapists enacting seven patterns makes clients recover**. Classification: **potential internal coverage test; not seven extra parts**.

**F03 — Refusal and defensive explanations.** Ch.4 and ch.6 of the edited book respect disliked/refused imagery and alternative paths, while ch.14 (Behary) sometimes frames refusal as a defense needing to be addressed. These cannot be flattened into one unified rule. The owner's guide expressly says refusing its frame is not a problem to solve and the model cannot force itself on the client. Classification: **preserve the owner's clearer refusal principle; audit examples for overinterpretation**.

**F04 — Three coping operations and process chain.** Van Genderen pp. 11–19, 42–58: need → schema/pitfall → surrender, avoidance or inversion → mode; see also tables pp.12–14. Outward toughness, clowning, perfectionism or excessive independence can defend against the same wound as withdrawal or compliance. These are hypotheses to examine, not a reliable test of undisclosed trauma. Classification: **convergent mechanism; one worked example might aid comprehension**.

**F05 — Helper repair.** Heath/Startup ch.11 demonstrates an actual therapist contribution to a rupture and negotiated repair; Van Genderen pp.141–144 includes acknowledgment/apology for mismanaging an appointment. The guide already covers internal repair and holding gurus/helpers accountable. Classification: **potential short practical therapist-selection criterion, subject to checking current master**.

**F06 — Sequencing disagreement is local, not field-wide.** Van Genderen presents five steps, assigning early weeks to identifying patterns before major change; some book instructions put off direct problem-solving. The guide prioritizes immediate practical safety when necessary, even before inner formulation. The same workbook also addresses crisis planning, external boundaries and behavior change, so do not portray Schema Therapy generally as anti-action. Classification: **difference of emphasis/protocol, not universal critique**.

**F07 — Broad emotional needs and life meaning not missing.** Van Genderen's expanded scheme includes fairness, coherent identity and meaningful world understanding; the guide already includes injustice, identity building, meaning and spirituality. Classification: **convergence**.

**F08 — Positive child and play are already clinical, too.** Heath/Startup ch.10 (Shaw) uses play, objects, group games and lightness; ch.4 stresses attuned play. Owner guide already uses creative activities, childhood objects and joyful reciprocity. Classification: **convergence, optional richer examples only**.

## New Pass 02 findings (F09–F17)

### F09 — Historical relationship: a close practical cousin, NOT demonstrably a pop derivation

**Historical evidence:** An early, sometimes coercive school of *reparenting* existed in transactional analysis by the 1960s–1970s. This must not be equated morally or clinically with the owner's consent-based self-reparenting. Later *inner child* books in popular self-help appeared alongside rather than downstream of modern Schema Therapy: John Bradshaw's *Homecoming* (original 1990); Lucia Capacchione's *Recovery of Your Inner Child* (1991). Jeffrey Young's first schema-focused therapy book appeared in **1989** (the International Society of Schema Therapy's bibliography states this); his comprehensive manual with Klosko and Weishaar appeared 2003. There is no verified evidence that Bradshaw or Capacchione derived their methods from Young, nor that Young's was copied from their work. **Convergent ancestry/practices, no proven influence direction.**

Sources verified: ISST bibliography `https://schematherapysociety.org/Books`; publisher `https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Recovery-of-Your-Inner-Child/Lucia-Capacchione/9780671701352`; Google Books 1990 Bradshaw `https://books.google.com/books/about/Homecoming.html?id=D2rsxgEACAAJ`; Transactional Analysis Journal critical retrospective doi:`10.1080/03621537.2021.2011025`; doi:`10.1080/03621537.2021.2011041`.

Capacchione is a **particularly high-priority unreviewed primary comparator**, because the publisher's detailed 1991 contents already list separate *Nurturing Parent*, *Protective Parent*, *Critical Parent*, *Vulnerable Child*, *Angry Child*, *Creative Child*, *Spiritual Child*, and non-dominant-hand writing. These overlap strongly with the owner's guide, which likewise contains Nurturer/Protector, child/critic, spiritual child and non-dominant-hand dialogue. That 1991 book merits a direct full reading before claims of unique framework roles. **Bibliography/toc inspected, book itself not yet read.**

Interpretive wording pending author approval: “Inner-child reparenting is a more accessible relative of the clinical methods developed in Schema Therapy; both also share ideas with older therapeutic and self-help traditions.” *Not* “self-reparenting is a watered-down Schema Therapy” or a demonstrated linear derivation.

### F10 — IREM clinical trial: meaningful evidence for a SPECIFIC professional technique

Edited volume ch.7 (Lee and colleagues) describes the IREM randomised trial of **imagery rescripting (ImRs) versus EMDR** for adults with childhood-trauma PTSD. The book was written before its trial results were published and **states it does not present results**. Therefore do not cite this chapter alone for efficacy.

Independently verified the published 2020 trial: Boterhoven de Haan et al., *British Journal of Psychiatry*, doi:`10.1192/bjp.2020.158`, PMID 32892758. 155 participants; up to 12 **therapist-delivered 90-minute sessions** twice weekly. Both interventions had large within-group symptom reductions at eight-week follow-up (d about 1.72 vs 1.73); there was **no statistically significant difference** between them; reported treatment dropout ~7.7%. This RCT had **no non-active randomized comparison arm** (some participants had a pre-treatment waiting observation); hence causal comparison against untreated recovery is more limited than an active-vs-control trial.

**Safety/transfer:** The trial excluded acute suicide risk, comorbid psychotic disorders, bipolar I, alcohol/drug dependence and some other conditions; professional assessment, consent and safety monitoring were provided. The chapter's “without prior stabilization” means no dedicated preparatory stabilization module in this screened clinical trial, **not** that everyone with dissociation/high risk should self-administer trauma imagery, nor that it overrides the guide's actual tolerance/consent gate. These outcomes are neither RCT validation of *complete* Schema Therapy nor validation of the owner's independent self-hypnosis/reparenting package.

Research conclusion: optional short evidence distinction for readers seeking a qualified trauma clinician; no automatic user-facing guide procedure based on RCT treatment scripts.

### F11 — Childhood memory is NOT required for all helpful imagery

Heath/Startup ch.8 (Maurer and Rafaeli) explicitly teaches imagery rescripting **of present-day events without locating childhood origin**, current-focused dialogue, and future-focused rehearsal. Modern/recent events can suffice when the immediate task is calming, differentiation, or Healthy Adult practice. These techniques are also offered as bridges for those unwilling to revisit historical material.

Owner guide already permits current-day protection without childhood interpretation or recovered memories; its language “The hook is not a truth detector” matches this. Added possibility: a worked, simple **future rehearsal** (prepare to say no / request care / handle pushback; respond from adult role; test real outcomes afterward). Its basic ingredients are already at `Make the Protector Visible` and `Borrow Hindsight`, so do not declare a missing innovation without checking usability.

### F12 — Vicarious learning without claiming a ready inner parent

Heath/Startup ch.9 (Farrell and Shaw) describes observing an adult/critic dialogue **before** the client is ready to enact the Healthy Adult, and using supporters/coaching of varying degrees. The text calls this vicarious learning, not proof of a pre-existing perfect Self. This maps extremely closely to the guide's `Borrow the Adult Before You Can Be the Adult`, `Borrow One Function at a Time`, and `The Adult Apprentice`.

One practical demonstration contrasts how encouragement versus punitive criticism affects a simple challenging task. This could inspire a **safe, entirely original optional learning exercise** (compare a short task under harsh vs supportive self-talk, without reenacting trauma or demanding measurable performance differences). However the chapter's anecdotal success is not trial evidence, demand/expectancy can influence results, and the exercise may encourage needless self-attack. Candidate **uncertain/low priority**, not automatically recommended.

### F13 — Last-step maintenance and relapse planning could improve usability

Van Genderen step 5, printed pp.214–230 (especially **225–226**), describes a compact relapse-prevention plan: recognize own triggers and unmet needs; activate care, reality-based appraisal and action; identify helpful people and professional recontact route; accept practical constraints; retain useful past notes. Future-oriented role play practices a boundary **despite imagined opposition** (pp.215–219). The book also describes reducing dependence on therapist and restoring equal friendship (pp.214, 227–229).

Guide already includes `One Intention in the Morning, One Review at Night`, `Make the Protector Visible`, `When to Change the Strategy`, `Borrowed Adulthood in Relationship`. Potential *reader value* is a **single optional crisis/recurrence menu that can be written while well** (e.g., signs / Nurturer step / Protector action / Guide decision / support person / when to stop inward work), rather than a newly prescribed tracking regimen. Avoid compulsive logging or treating a recurrence as proof the method failed.

### F14 — A supportive relationship can properly outlast a particular caregiver

Heath/Startup ch.20 (Vanko and Roberts) describes internalizing the therapist's reliable presence, transitional objects, and the Healthy Adult learning to mobilize external help. It explicitly notes that **limited reparenting can be shared among more than one caregiver/helper**. The guide already advocates a network of several supports, symbolic reminders and reclaiming autonomy, rather than complete aloneness. This **disconfirms** any claim that clinical Schema Therapy necessarily creates one-person dependency. The chapter also directly addresses unwanted early endings as possible therapist-caused harm.

Classification: **strong convergence, not a new difference**. Consider therapist repair and exit planning for reader seeking a provider.

### F15 — Couples and practical relational reciprocity already have formal Schema Therapy analogues

Heath/Startup ch.13 (Atkinson and Perris) describes two partners' mode cycles, mutual Healthy Adult attention, and reparenting dialogues between partners. Therefore “my guide extends reparenting outside solo inner work and into relationships” is not *per se* unique. A complete couples therapy model, however, is not an obligation for a lay person's general guide. Class: **overlap; future specialist source only if couples appendix desired**.

### F16 — A genuine unresolved clinical tension: how much trauma-history interpretation to prompt

In van Genderen's self-help book, steps 1–3 encourage present-feeling-to-childhood links and intense imagery; it also says exact historic recall is not the focus. The owner's guide explicitly separates current emotional truth from historical fact and prohibits treating an induced image as proof of an event; the user can decline framing. A number of experiential techniques might therefore *fit the emotional function* yet be **incompatible as literal unsupervised scripts** if presented as recovered memory work.

Heath/Startup ch.8's current-focused imagery provides a credible **alternative path** without taking away self-directed reparenting itself.

### F17 — Two separate scientific validity planes

An RCT for a specialist trauma intervention does not validate every element of Schema Therapy; an RCT for Schema Therapy does not validate all popular inner-child techniques; evidence for a *component* of the owner's guide does not validate the full blended package. Likewise, a book's clinician illustrations show how a method is *performed*, not that it reliably works across populations. Separate (a) theoretical resemblance, (b) clinical feasibility, (c) efficacy tested in specified samples/interventions/outcomes, and (d) personal practice reports.
