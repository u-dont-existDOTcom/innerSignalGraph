## Evidence map

| Cluster | Strongest bounded evidence | What it supports | Important limits for A001 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fear of compassion / affiliative threat | Gilbert et al. developed measures of fear of compassion for others, from others, and for self, and related these fears to self-criticism, attachment, and distress ([2011 primary study](https://doi.org/10.1348/147608310X526511)). A clinical-population CFT review included 15 randomized or randomized pilot studies ([Millard et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36649790/)). | Compassion can be available in principle yet cue fear or resistance; introducing more warmth is not automatically experienced as safe. Fears/blocks should be explored rather than treated as refusal or deficiency. | The 2011 paper is scale development and association, not a test of the proposed credibility sequence. The CFT review found promising effects but unclear quality in 7/15 studies, a predominantly female sample, and limited long-term evidence. It does not show that regulation alone repairs relational credibility. |
| Self-compassion and self-criticism | A review of 20 RCTs found a medium reduction in self-criticism versus controls ([Wakelin et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33749936/)); CFT-specific meta-analysis also found reductions but emphasized the small evidence base ([Vidal & Soldevilla, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36172899/)). | Directly addressing self-criticism and building self-reassurance can help across interventions. | Aggregate reduction in self-criticism does not establish that the first useful move is “be compassionate,” that compassion will feel safe, or that the owner’s credibility concern has been repaired. Many comparisons are passive, heterogeneous, and delivered over much more than one response. |
| Schema modes and limited reparenting | An RCT process analysis found Healthy Adult and Vulnerable Child modes central to change across schema therapy, clarification-oriented psychotherapy, and treatment as usual ([Yakın et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brat.2020.103553)). A large BPD trial found combined individual/group schema therapy superior to treatment as usual over a two-year intervention ([Arntz et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35234828/)); a review found evidence strongest in personality disorders and sparse elsewhere ([Taylor et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28035734/)). | It is clinically intelligible to distinguish vulnerable, punitive/critical, angry, coping, and healthier adult states and to strengthen care plus limit-setting. Limited reparenting emphasizes meeting unmet needs within relationship boundaries. | Mode labels are a therapeutic model, not discovered facts about a speaker. The strongest outcomes concern diagnosed personality-disorder populations, clinician-delivered treatment, and long time horizons. Limited reparenting is primarily a therapist–client relationship intervention; translating it into self-reparenting or AI language is an adaptation, not direct evidence. |
| Emotion-focused two-chair work | A nine-client pilot found reductions in self-criticism and increases in self-compassion/self-reassurance after emotion-focused two-chair dialogue ([Shahar et al., 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21710579/)). A 2024 seven-patient chairwork pilot reported feasibility and improvement while explicitly calling for larger RCTs ([Kroener et al., 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39011336/)). | Separating a criticizing position from the experiencing target can make resentment and its feared burden hearable without allowing the critic to dominate the whole conversation. The exercise can be offered as a perspective-taking structure rather than a claim that literal autonomous parts exist. | Evidence is preliminary, small, clinician-guided, and multi-session. Chairwork can feel artificial; A001 must accept rejection of “parts” framing and offer ordinary-language alternatives. It does not by itself supply the credibility experiment or age/agency analysis. |
| Alliance rupture recognition and repair | A meta-analysis of 11 studies/1,314 patients found a moderate association between rupture resolution and positive treatment outcome, while rupture-repair training across six studies did not show a significant outcome relation ([Eubanks et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30335462/)). A broad alliance meta-analysis also stresses association and causality limits ([Flückiger et al., 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29792475/)). | When trust or collaboration breaks, recognize the break, invite the injured party’s experience, and adjust goals/tasks instead of pressing the same intervention. This supports a non-defensive response to “big fuckity whoopty doo.” | This research concerns the patient–therapist alliance. Applying it to an internal relationship is an analogy; it does not prove an “inner rupture” mechanism or that the AI can occupy a therapist role. Even the human treatment literature does not establish simple causality. |
| Behavioral experiments and explicit prediction testing | In a randomized nonclinical single-session study of 91 participants, both thought records and behavioral experiments improved beliefs versus control, with some earlier/broader change for behavioral experiments; the experiment was collaboratively built from a specific prediction and reviewed afterward ([McManus et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbtep.2011.07.003)). | Ask for a falsifiable prediction, select a small observable action collaboratively, specify timing/completion, observe what happens, and review the meaning rather than asking for trust in advance. | The study concerned anxious beliefs in a nonclinical sample, not accumulated relational credibility. A completed action is one data point, not proof of trust or repair. The no-arrears missed-promise rule is a project hypothesis, not an established behavioral-experiment finding. |
| Imagery rescripting | An updated meta-analysis of 17 RCTs/908 participants found benefit versus passive controls and no significant difference versus several active interventions for disorders involving aversive memories ([Kip et al., 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37699277/)). | If a later A001 turn centers on a specific aversive memory and changed meaning, imagery rescripting is an established candidate rather than something to reinvent. | The original A001 message is primarily about present credibility and blame, not a request to revisit one memory. Rescripting can be emotionally activating and is unnecessary in the first response. The evidence is condition-specific and does not validate spontaneous AI-led memory work. |
| Conversational AI transfer | A 2025 review/meta-analysis of 14 GenAI-chatbot RCTs found a small aggregate effect with a very wide prediction interval and moderate risk of bias ([Zhang et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41401240/)). A 2026 university-student RCT reported symptom changes and associations between perceived alliance, engagement, and improvement, with declared commercial conflicts ([Shoshani et al., 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41979879/)). A 2026 safety review found uneven safeguards, limited oversight/adverse-event monitoring, and documented missed suicidality or inaccurate clinical information ([scoping review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42194487/)). | Conversational systems can be experienced as warm/collaborative and can support structured interventions, so testing the actual production interaction is reasonable. | No located study validates this exact A001 sequence, internal-role formulation, one-message usefulness, missed-promise repair, or transfer of human alliance rupture theory to self-dialogue. Perceived digital alliance is not proof that the model understands, is a therapist, or can safely lead complex memory work. |

## Existing-work map

### Already explained by established work

- Care or compassion can itself evoke threat, avoidance, anger, or distrust; more soothing is not automatically the answer.
- Self-criticism can be addressed directly rather than treated as noise around vulnerability.
- Differentiating experiential positions and strengthening a healthier adult function are recognizable therapeutic strategies.
- Collaborative prediction testing is an established way to replace assertion with observable evidence.
- Human therapeutic ruptures merit direct recognition and task/goal renegotiation.
- Imagery rescripting is established for some aversive-memory problems, but is not automatically indicated here.

### Directly adaptable components

- Validate fear of receiving care and ask what makes care unsafe.
- Treat role labels as optional hypotheses; use the user’s language and allow “this does not feel like parts.”
- Give resentment a separate, non-shaming hearing while setting a firm boundary against contempt and retaliation.
- Elicit one explicit prediction and collaboratively choose one small, observable, time-bounded action.
- Review the outcome without treating either skepticism or one success as infallible.
- Recognize interactional mismatch and revise the task rather than repeating the same vow or relaxation exercise.

### Composable but not directly proven

- “Credibility rupture” composes CFT affiliative threat, rupture recognition, and behavioral experimentation; it is a useful formulation hypothesis, not an established branded treatment.
- The three-position structure—skeptical younger-facing state, resentful/blaming position, apprentice adult function—adapts schema/EFT differentiation without asserting literal autonomous people.
- Asking the skeptical side to help select the test combines collaboration and prediction testing; direct evidence for this precise internal negotiation was not found.
- Repair-and-resize after a missed promise follows non-defensive rupture principles, but the explicit ban on punitive “care arrears” remains an A001-specific hypothesis requiring trajectory and owner testing.

### Incompatible or deliberately excluded from the first response

- Generic relaxation as the lead intervention: it may regulate arousal but does not address the stated credibility failure.
- Categorical “protector,” “Punitive Parent,” “Vulnerable Child,” or inherited-parent assignments: these would turn therapeutic models into unsupported facts.
- Immediate imagery rescripting or deep memory work: the original request does not establish a specific target memory or readiness.
- Grand reparenting vows or claims that trust/healing occurred: these repeat the credibility problem and exceed the evidence.

### Genuinely unresolved / experiment required

- Which composition and pacing creates immediate usefulness for this owner in one production response.
- How much age/agency clarification belongs in the first message versus the next turn.
- Whether direct differentiation/chairwork feels clarifying or artificial.
- Whether the collaborative micro-commitment feels credible rather than managerial.
- Whether the missed-promise/no-arrears rule survives the required trajectory and owner review.
- Whether the best response should name “credibility rupture” or simply embody the idea in ordinary language.

## Therapist or different-level-of-care boundary

This scan does not justify an AI diagnosing trauma, personality disorder, dissociation, or a schema mode. Human clinical support is materially different when there is current danger, severe functional impairment, destabilizing dissociation, escalating self-harm/suicide risk, intense trauma-memory work, or a need for assessment and sustained relational repair. Those boundaries belong in routing and follow-up when actually triggered; they should not be appended as irrelevant boilerplate to the original A001 answer.

## Reuse decision

Disposition: `compose`.

Use established component mechanics while preserving the owner’s independent conception:

- CFT: care can cue threat; do not force compassion or equate it with safety.
- Schema/EFT: differentiate positions and support healthier adult functioning without reifying labels.
- Rupture repair: acknowledge the credibility break and revise the interaction non-defensively; treat the internal transfer as analogy only.
- CBT behavioral experiment: explicit prediction, collaborative test, observable timing, and later review.
- Imagery rescripting: retain only as a later conditional option if a specific memory becomes central.

The project-specific remainder is the natural, low-burden composition, A001 longitudinal handling, missed-promise/no-arrears hypothesis, and exact production implementation. These must be settled by comparative execution, blind filtering, and owner experiential judgment—not by inventing a new therapy brand or inflating adjacent evidence.
